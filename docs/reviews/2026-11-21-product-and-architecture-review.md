# Synlynk from Project Pulse to the public developer preview

**Product, market and architecture review | November 21, 2026**

**Recommendation. Keep the public developer preview open. Make Core + Personal general availability contingent on a trustworthy first job, a lossless upgrade, and a truthful task receipt.** The strongest initial customer already runs two coding agents and acts as the courier between them. Give that person a way to hand off a real task, leave the terminal, and return to a reviewable change with its cost, evidence and next owner intact. That addresses the problem that produced Synlynk. It also gives a buyer a practical reason to adopt another tool.

The project already distributes a working Python package. Its strongest technical assets are the local state ledger, separation of role from execution harness, explicit evidence of job effects, and machinery for independent review. Its largest liability is the distance between a policy described in the docs and the data flowing through the production path that implements it. The empirical router and the derived roadmap both illustrate this. Managed infrastructure would multiply the impact of these discrepancies.

## 2. Who would use it and who would pay

| Segment | User and buyer | Trigger and first useful outcome | Adoption or purchase test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent multi-harness developer or maintainer | Same person operates and pays | Switch from one agent to another after a quota limit or failed attempt. Recover the task, constraints and change with one command. | Can complete a second real task unaided in the same week. Free Core or Personal with own credentials. |
| Small product engineering team | Developers and reviewers use it; engineering lead chooses it | A queue of bounded issues spans multiple contributors or agents. Assign work, inspect a diff, verify independently and hand over at shift end. | Lower reviewer minutes per accepted PR at the same defect rate. Pay for shared oversight, recovery and reduced coordination time. |
| Software agency or delivery consultancy | Delivery lead and account owner buy; engineers operate | Several client repositories share a team. Produce an attributable change and a per-client cost statement without mixing client context. | Reconcile receipts to invoices and demonstrate client isolation. First plausible paid design partner. |
| Platform engineering, developer productivity and FinOps | Platform lead buys; staff engineers and finance consume evidence | Multiple teams use paid providers with inconsistent policies. Explain a routing choice, compare accepted outcomes and audit authority. | Cost provenance, versioned policies, export and minimum sample sizes survive an internal audit. |
| Regulated engineering organization | Engineering sponsor, procurement and security approve | Support internal development on approved data, with traceable human acceptance and reproducible recovery. | Private deployment, identity integration, retention settings and an independently reviewed isolation model. Start with low-risk internal tooling. |
| Creative studio, agency producer or research operations lead | Producer or account director buys; contributors approve assets | Move a brief through revisions and approvals across humans and agents. Existing studio and agency packs supply role vocabulary. | Demonstrate an accepted artifact in the team's production tool, with version identity and sign-off. A renamed software role is insufficient. [Pack implementation](../../synlynk/types_registry.py) |
| Business owner buying a delivered outcome | Sponsor approves scope and acceptance; operator runs the fleet | Submit a bounded change with an executable acceptance criterion. Receive a change, review trail and quote. | Repeat engagements with positive contribution margin after support, review, retries and failed runs. Treat Tier 3 as a service pilot. [IBPD tiers](../../synlynk/ibpd.py) |

The weakest initial fits are occasional single-agent users with no handoff cost, teams whose primary artifacts live outside versioned systems, and buyers that need an unattended operator for consequential production changes. Their integration or assurance burden consumes the value of the first deployment.

## 3. Industry and functional fit

These are **analyst judgments of present product fit**, not measured ROI or market size. Each determinant runs from 1 to 5. **W** is frequency of repeatable multi-step work, **V** is ease of independently verifying completion, **H** is value of cross-harness or human handoff, **I** is fit with existing repository, GitHub and CLI integrations, and **A** is ease of adoption given permissions, operational risk and procurement. Five is favorable on every axis. The total is four times the sum. Equal weighting makes each assumption visible; a failed mandatory control still disqualifies a deployment.

The best commercial pilot can score below the best usability fit. A consultancy can attach a price to reviewer time and client reporting. An open-source maintainer is a better test of unaided adoption. Recruit both and evaluate them on different outcomes.

## 5. Architecture and operational review

### A task's path through the system

1. **Intent and authority.** A person enters through the CLI, wizard or Vizor. Intents, goals and stories describe why work exists. A role is the accountable function, such as QA. A harness is an execution backend, such as Codex. This separation allows a reviewer to retain distinct authority even when the organization changes model providers. Edition, capability and GitHub identity checks must hold at the action boundary as well as in generated instructions. [Intent capture](../../synlynk/intent.py) · [Role and harness glossary](../glossary-agent-vs-harness.md) · [Governance gate](../../synlynk/governs_gate.py)
2. **Admission and scheduling.** The scheduler considers eligible work and available capacity. Dispatch resolves a harness under policy, checks permissions and prepares a worktree. Context packs and generated instructions give the child the project's constraints. Worktrees isolate Git changes. Credentials, network access and external tool permissions need separate controls. [Scheduler](../../synlynk/scheduler.py) · [Dispatch stages](../../synlynk/dispatch_pipeline.py) · [Adapter interface](../../synlynk/harness_adapters/base.py)
3. **Execution.** The agent runs as an external process with harness-specific flags, environment, logs and quota behavior. A task receipt can confirm delivery of the prompt. A real diff, a passing test and a confirmed remote effect are stronger and different observations. Operator actions include cancellation, retry, handoff and approval. Each action crosses process, filesystem or remote API boundaries. [Dispatch](../../synlynk/dispatch.py) · [Job lifecycle](../../synlynk/jobs.py)
4. **Settlement and learning.** Shared SQLite stores job evidence, cost records, ratings and decisions. WAL allows independent worktree processes to coordinate. Immutable effect contracts state what a job is expected to do; canonical decisions and an outbox support replay and observation. The canonical decision path defaults to shadow mode in the inspected projection. A successful child exit alone cannot substantiate a merge or a dollar charge. [Job truth](../../synlynk/job_truth.py) · [Status projection](../../synlynk/job_status_projection.py) · [Routing](../../synlynk/routing.py)
5. **Projection and collaboration.** Markdown, CLI status, Vizor and GitHub issues and milestones expose different views of local state. Relay extends coordination between devices. These surfaces cross trust and consistency boundaries. Local data, cached remote data, provider-reported usage and actual financial settlement need explicit provenance in every projection. The observatory's local server binds to loopback; moving that surface into a hosted account introduces tenant authentication and authorization requirements. [Vizor server](../../synlynk/viz/server.py) · [GitHub synchronization](../../synlynk/gh_sync.py) · [Personal relay](../personal-hosted-relay.md)

**Preserve the local core.** A stdlib-only Python CLI, optional networking dependencies, a real adapter interface and SQLite online backup keep operation and recovery intelligible to a small team. The October WAL soak recorded all 400 expected transactions from 50 writing threads with eight readers, zero lock errors, and successful integrity and reopen checks. Separate tests exercise idempotent evidence, outbox rollback and concurrent writers. These are valuable, bounded results. They say little about version migration, duplicate external writes or long-running tenant isolation. [Package manifest](../../pyproject.toml) · [WAL results](../../project-docs/reports/2026-10-08-state-db-wal-soak.md) · [Evidence tests](../../tests/test_job_truth.py) · [Backup](../../synlynk/backup.py)

### Findings that change release decisions

Severity reflects impact on the advertised workflow. Reproductions used temporary SQLite databases and production functions unless explicitly marked as inspection or preview. They establish reachable behavior under the stated conditions. Field incidence remains unknown.

| Priority and affected contract | Evidence at 9582a9d5 | Remediation and closing test |
|---|---|---|
| **High — upgrading preserves the roadmap** | A valid legacy standalone arc and phase became zero arcs and zero phases after the full migrator ran. The conversion updates existing goals that already have a phase ID, then drops the source tables. A person who upgrades can lose information from the active roadmap. [Conversion](../../synlynk/db_schema.py#L849) · [Migration entry](../../synlynk/db.py#L2623) | Backfill orphan arcs, phases, identities and metadata. Verify complete old-to-new mappings transactionally before replacing tables. Restore a copy of a populated old workspace and compare keys, titles, targets, links and counts. Block upgrade or provide verified recovery when parity fails. |
| **High — a milestone keeps its identity** | A stored mapping for milestone 41 joined v2.0 initially and v1.0 after inserting that earlier version. The view exposes a row number ordered by version. Main verifies the live title before one PATCH, while story backfill still consumes the positional join. [View](../../synlynk/db_schema.py#L669) · [Latest guard](../../synlynk/gh_sync.py#L161) · [Story lookup](../../synlynk/gh_sync_backfill.py#L35) | Persist an immutable identity, migrate existing mappings with remote identity checks and quarantine ambiguity. | **High — first job runs on the selected harness with the confirmed context** | A fresh first-task dry-run with OpenRouter, Codex or Cursor selected separately presented the same three Claude tasks. | **High — empirical routing uses real completed work** | Five implementation jobs with backend stories produced five backend ratings and five cost rows. Recovery compares an ownership token without expiry. [Claim](../../synlynk/jobs.py#L3767) · [Reconciliation](../../synlynk/jobs.py#L5947) | Add a generation-bound, expiring lease. Recheck liveness and job evidence before cleanup. Kill the owner before and after each side effect and verify safe reclamation. |

**Maintainability.** A source count under the pinned package found 315 Python files and 123,131 physical lines, including embedded HTML. Four functions concentrate changes across otherwise useful boundaries. The CLI parser spans 2,018 lines, CLI main 1,868, dispatch_agent 1,740 and legacy migration/repair 1,328. Dispatch already delegates resolution and worktree preparation, then spawns the process and records lifecycle state through separate JSON and SQLite paths. An interruption between those steps can leave consumers with different views. Move one transition at a time behind a transactional job interface. Start with claim/reserve, spawn acknowledgment and settlement. Project JSON from committed events, keep existing readers compatible, and compare results under crash injection before retiring a writer. A wholesale services rewrite would add failure boundaries before repairing these. [CLI](../../synlynk/cli.py#L383) · [Dispatch](../../synlynk/dispatch.py#L4649) · [Database compatibility](../../synlynk/db.py#L1151)

**Measurement quality.** The October benchmark reports substantial internal fleet use and a calibration exercise. Its spend-per-task table divides observed spend by dispatched tasks. That denominator cannot measure cost per accepted fix without acceptance and rework data. The November snapshot contains 4,135 ratings, 160 explicitly quality-verified, 156 explicitly unverified and 3,819 with no value for that flag. This does not invalidate all historical runs. It limits the claims that can be made about independently verified quality. Publish coverage, failures, timeouts, missing costs, subscription allocation and actual-versus-API-equivalent spend. Compare matched task classes within repository and time window. Release a public index only after testing whether rankings generalize beyond this project. ## 7. Recommended release roadmap

These are proposed release gates and success targets, not committed ship dates. The November 20 local decision record favors narrowed native skills in v0.39, Core + Personal in v0.40, site/support in .41, identity/billing in .42, connected Vizor/backup in .43 and managed infrastructure in .44–.47. That record was untracked in the supplied checkout. The ledger also places a Hetzner arc at .39.1 and native skills at .45.0, while main is already landing first-run fixes. Reconcile the competing assignments publicly in one authoritative roadmap before announcing dates. Keep only skills that the first successful task needs on the critical path.

## 8. Product improvements in priority order

1. **Make the receipt the main user-facing object.** One command and one shareable view should reconstruct the task, selected and actual harness/model, permissions, context version, diff, tests, remote outcome, reviewer, elapsed time and cost provenance. Show unknowns explicitly. Let a developer resume from a failed receipt or hand it to another operator.
2. **Make evidence travel in both directions.** Start from a goal and find the released change. Start from a PyPI version and find the intention, approval, effect and upgrade evidence. Store stable goal, story, job, PR and release identifiers on events. The 22/109 strict-join result gives a baseline that can improve without manually backfilling every historical anecdote.
3. **Give the router a reproducible comparison.** Run a small held-out suite and real opt-in shadow comparisons against the default harness. Stratify implementation, review and test work. Report accepted changes per dollar, user wait time, reviewer minutes, rework and the share of jobs with verified evidence. Promote a policy only when it improves a stated outcome with sufficient coverage.
4. **Make the smallest useful configuration first-class.** One repo, one harness, one modest task and one human reviewer should feel complete. Reveal role policies, cloud services, Studio and custom industry packs when a second user, provider or workspace creates the need.
5. **Test the boundaries where the product makes promises.** Package-to-first-job, old-database-to-new-roadmap, job-completion-to-router, retry-to-billing and approval-to-remote-write each need an end-to-end contract test. Unit-test quantity and static HTML checks cannot substitute for these journeys.
6. **Use independent pilots to choose the next market.** Measure a single maintainer's unaided repeat use, a team's change in reviewer minutes and an agency's cost to produce an accepted client deliverable. If multi-harness switching is infrequent but receipts consistently save time, lead with verified handoff and keep routing as supporting infrastructure. If customers value shared state but avoid hosted execution, prioritize portable backup and team policy before compute.

**Decision for the next release meeting.** Approve the maintenance and first-job gates, name owners for migration and routing data contracts, and recruit external pilot operators before adding more release themes. The first result worth announcing is an unaided user completing a real task on their chosen provider, upgrading without losing state, and handing another person a receipt they can verify.
