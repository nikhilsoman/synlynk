Decision: Phase 1 adopts, as governing principle, that no harness-specific instruction file may hard-pin a role, task-type lane, or privilege level — role is a uniform per-task selection mechanism resolved identically at dispatch time across Claude, Agy, Grok, Codex, and Muse, consistent with the 2026-08-30 harness/role separation design; today's incident (Claude's CLAUDE.md welding `pm, review, deploy` onto the harness, with no path to the `qa` identity that actually held merge authority) is the confirmed violation, and the panel finds no evidence yet on whether other harnesses' instruction files carry an equivalent lock — that is precisely what the commissioned audit must determine, not an assumption to carry forward. On Q3, the panel rejects both pure resurrection and pure permanent replacement: the 2026-08-09 holdback design's 2–10% exploration bands are the wrong instrument for a mandatory-coverage objective and are discarded for the current phase, but its ledger schema — keyed `(role, task-type, SFIA skill)` with confidence-adaptive thresholds and canaried re-ranking — is retained as the eventual steady-state mechanism; concretely, mandatory round-robin becomes the active sampling distribution now, with holdback-style adaptive sampling reinstated per-cell only once that cell clears its sample-size bar (the panel sets this at ≥10 observations, aligning the empirical policy's ≥5 "may-influence-routing" floor with the holdback design's own ≥10 "stop exploring randomly" threshold, rather than treating these as competing numbers). The Capability-Based Task Allocation table is found to structurally prevent ever collecting drift-relevant samples for lanes no harness is assigned, and is to be retired as a routing authority — demoted to advisory-only — once round-robin is implemented in Phase 2; it is not extended or treated as a continuing interim default beyond that. Round-robin-induced failures (Agy timeouts, Grok quota exhaustion) get one bounded retry on the same harness, then rotation to the next harness in ring with the failure logged as a ledger sample rather than silently absorbed; any deviation from round-robin requires a written ledger exception reason reviewed by PM, so convenience-based reversion cannot occur by default omission. The panel also flags, as a finding to carry into Phase 2 without acting on it now (out of scope this session), that preflight-only enforcement is already demonstrated insufficient — three `GOVERNS_PHASE_MISSING` hits today were each resolved by bypassing dispatch entirely via direct `git`/`gh` calls, so action-layer enforcement remains necessary even though implementing it is explicitly deferred. Two investigations are commissioned: (1) a harness-parity trial running an equivalent small task through Claude, Agy, Grok, Codex, and Muse in both interactive and headless modes, logging gate-hits, required handoffs, elapsed time, and cost; and (2) an instruction-file-and-config uniformity audit covering `CLAUDE.md`, `AI_INSTRUCTIONS.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `GEMINI.md`, `.cursorrules`, `.synlynk/policy.json`'s `task_allocation`/`merge_authority` blocks, and `.synlynk/config.json`'s per-harness sections, to determine whether the role-lock found in Claude's file today is unique to Claude or present — consistently or inconsistently — elsewhere. All four panelists concur; no dissent recorded.
